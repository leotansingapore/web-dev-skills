# Designing the curriculum

The manifest is the product. Write it before any lesson, by hand, and treat every slug as a URL you will live with.

## Tracks: one per audience, named by the job

Start from the product's primary users, not from topics. SearchBlueprint's constitution names business owners, agency operators and their clients; the competitor had general, Shopify and local hubs. The union became four tracks: **SEO + AI Search** (founders, small teams), **Local SEO** (anyone serving a place), **E-commerce SEO** (any store platform, not one vendor), **Agency SEO** (the audience the competitor ignored). "For all" means every audience the product sells to has a track whose first lesson speaks to their situation.

Per track record: `title`, `shortTitle`, `audience` (one plain sentence), `promise` (one sentence, the outcome), `description` (two sentences, what the chapters cover), `startHere` (the lesson slug to feature).

## Chapters: 8–10, in reading order, mechanism first

Reading order for someone starting cold. The archetypes that recur across every successful hub, in the order that teaches:

1. **How it works** — the mechanics of the channel (how Google ranks, how AI assistants cite, how local search weighs relevance/distance/prominence).
2. **Find the targets** — keywords, intent, mapping to pages.
3. **Structure** — architecture, internal links, indexing, speed.
4. **Content** — the page or asset that wins the target.
5. **AI search** — being cited and recommended, and measuring it.
6. **Authority** — links, mentions, reviews, entity signals.
7. **Measure** — the reports, read honestly.
8. **Run it with an AI assistant** — connect the product's MCP or API to live data; a weekly routine; a 30-minute audit.
9. **The plan** — a phased 90-day sequence that links back to every chapter.

Vertical tracks keep the same spine but rename each chapter in the audience's nouns (store: collections and product pages; local: profile, location pages, reviews, citations; agency: scoping, audits that sell, production, reporting, retention).

Per chapter record: `slug`, `title` (a promise or an action, sentence case), `blurb` (one or two sentences: what the reader can do after it).

## Lessons: 2–4 per chapter, one idea each

A lesson is one mechanism and what to do about it, 700–1,100 words, with a single action at the end. Per lesson record: `slug` (stable, descriptive, no dates), `title` (sentence case; a statement or a question answered), `summary` (one sentence, the outcome the reader leaves with — this is the card copy and the meta description).

Sizing that worked: 32 + 30 + 27 + 22 = 111 lessons across four tracks. Match the competitor's order of magnitude per track; beat them on action-per-lesson and on honesty, not on raw count.

## Naming rules

- Slugs: kebab-case, no stop words where avoidable, no numbers unless they are the subject (`days-1-to-30-foundations`).
- Titles: the reader's words, not the industry's ("Why some stores rank and yours doesn't", not "E-commerce ranking factors").
- Avoid a title that promises a result ("Rank in 30 days"); promise understanding or a deliverable.

## Manifest text is public copy: track promises, chapter blurbs and lesson summaries render on the hub and track pages, so they obey the same rules as lesson prose — no unsourced numbers, no capability the product doesn't have, no hype vocabulary.

## The test of a good manifest

Read only the titles and summaries top to bottom. If it reads as a course a careful practitioner would teach, in that order, with no lesson you could not write honestly, it is done. If a lesson needs a statistic you cannot source to be interesting, rewrite the summary around the mechanism instead.
